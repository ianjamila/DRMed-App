Two gates you will bump into

The system enforces two rules in the database itself. No button, role or password gets around them, so it is worth knowing them by heart.

Lab gate
A test does not appear on the lab bench until the visit is paid, waived, or billed to an HMO. Until then it sits in the reception queue under Waiting for payment, and the lab sees an awaiting payment chip on the results archive. An HMO-billed visit never waits there — it is settled money as far as both gates are concerned, so it goes straight to Processing.
Release gate
A finished result cannot be released to the patient until the visit is paid, waived, or billed to an HMO. Since the September 2026 release both gates use the same rule, so an HMO visit that reached the bench also reaches the patient: the clinic books the claim as a receivable the moment the result is released, and collects from the HMO months later. A blocked line reads Visit must be paid, waived, or HMO-covered before release.

The life of a test

Every test line on a visit moves along this ladder. The words are the ones you will see in status chips.

requested→ in progress→ ready for release→ released
requested
Reception has billed it. Nobody has picked it up yet.
in progress
A medtech or x-ray tech has claimed it and is working on it.
handed back
Not a status — an amber chip under the status on the visit page (Admin and lab staff). It means someone claimed the test and then put it back in the queue; ×2 means it happened twice. Hover over it for who did it and why; the test page's Claim history has the full story.
ready for release
Finalised. Reception can release it once the visit is paid.
released
The patient can see and download it in the portal.
cancelled / deleted
Removed from the queue by reception before payment. Nothing is billed for it, and it can be restored.
Also

A status called result uploaded exists for tests that require a pathologist's sign-off before release. No service uses it today and the sign-off screen is not yet built. Leave Requires sign-off off on every service until it is. See Known limitations.

The life of a visit's money

unpaid
Nothing recorded yet. The visit can still be deleted from the queue.
partial
Some payment recorded, balance outstanding.
paid
Fully settled. Lab and release both open.
waived
Admin decided not to collect. Treated as paid. Cannot be deleted.

A payment is never erased. Edit fixes a wrong method, amount or reference; Delete takes a payment off the visit. Either way the old record stays in the visit's history with the reason and who did it, and the books correct themselves. Every edit, delete, release, print and download is written to the audit log, because the Data Privacy Act (RA 10173) requires it.

Three identifiers

DRM-0001
The patient's permanent DRM-ID. Assigned once, printed on every receipt, used to sign in to the portal.
Visit #
A running number per visit. Patients quote it when they call. Search it with or without leading zeros.
Secure PIN
Eight characters, issued per visit, valid 60 days, shown on the printed receipt only until it is printed. A new visit issues its own new PIN without cancelling an earlier one — a patient can legitimately hold more than one valid PIN at once, from different visits, each expiring on its own 60-day clock. Re-issuing a lost PIN replaces that one visit's PIN in place; it doesn't touch any other visit's.

Getting started

Who: every staff memberWhere: drmed.ph/staff

2.1 Signing in

  1. Open drmed.ph/staff/login.

    Patients use a different door, drmed.ph/portal. Don't mix them up when helping someone at the counter.

  2. Click Continue with Google and pick your work Google account.

    You land on the dashboard for your role. Signing in with email and password still works below the divider, as a fallback for when Google is unavailable.

No reset link

There is no self-service Forgot password for staff. If you sign in with Google, use Google's own account recovery. For the password fallback, ask an Admin to reset it under Staff Users. Too many wrong attempts in a row lock sign-in for a few minutes.

2.2 Two-step sign-in (MFA)

A 6-digit code from an authenticator app on top of your password. Optional for every role, including Admins — signing in with Google already gives you your second step. Once you set it up it is asked for on every sign-in, Google included.

  1. After signing in, open My Profile under Personal and click Set up two-step sign-in.

    It takes you to /staff/mfa. The panel there tells you where you stand: Off — add a one-time code from your phone as a second sign-in step. or, once you are set up, On — a one-time code from your authenticator app is required at sign-in.

  2. Scan the QR code with Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator, 1Password or similar.

  3. Type the 6-digit code the app shows and verify.

Lost phone

There are no recovery codes, no self-service reset, and no screen anywhere in the app — not even an Admin's — that removes an enrolled authenticator. Once you're enrolled the code is required on every sign-in, including Google — losing the phone does not let you skip it. Clearing the stale factor still needs someone with access to the Supabase project. Enrol on a phone you will keep, and consider adding the same account to a second device (most authenticator apps allow it) while you set it up. See Known limitations.

Sorting and paging, on every list

Wherever the app shows a list of records, the same two controls work the same way. Click a column heading to sort by it; click it again to reverse the order. A small arrow marks the column currently in use. At the bottom, Rows picks how many rows a page holds (5 / 10 / 25 / 50 / 100) and the line beside it reads, for example, Showing 1–25 of 300 services — so you always know how many records matched, not just how many you can see.

The screens with sorting and paging are: Patients, Appointments, Visit Records, Services, Inventory, Website Messages, Gift Codes, Critical Alerts (the acknowledged list), Users, Chart of Accounts, Vendor Bills, Bill Payments, Vendors, Pay Doctors › Already paid, Lab queue, Results, Audit Log, Emails Sent, Journal Entries, Patient AR aging, Newsletter (the subscriber list), HMO Claims (the All unbilled and All aging tables), the payroll lists (Pay Periods, Run Payroll, Employees, Government Rates (SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG, Tax), Holidays, Leaves and Overtime Slips), employee allowances/loans/history, run employees and DTR imports/reconciliation, and five Admin reports — Deleted Queue Entries, Undone Releases, Stuck Tests, Staff Cash Advances and Patients Without Consent. Closures sorts but has no pager — it is only ever a handful of rows, and the same is true of the Outstanding by staff summary at the top of Staff Cash Advances, which sorts independently of the advances ledger below it.

Two things are worth knowing about the columns. A heading with no arrow on hover cannot be sorted, and that is deliberate: it usually means the column is worked out after the rows are fetched — Tests on Visit Records, Type (DR) and Amount on Journal Entries, Recipient, Email and Status on Emails Sent, Patient on the Lab queue and Results, and Reason on Deleted Queue Entries and Undone Releases (free text, so an alphabetical order would tell you nothing). And on HMO Claims the sort and rows-per-page are not kept in the address bar the way they are elsewhere, because that screen loads its rows once and keeps your ticked checkboxes while you page.

Sorting and the row count live in the address bar, so a sorted list can be bookmarked or pasted to a colleague and it opens the same way for them. Changing a filter, a sort or the rows-per-page always returns you to page 1, so you are never left staring at an empty page 7 of a list that just got shorter. Switching a tab or a filter chip keeps the sort you chose — the columns are the same, only the set is narrower.

Two search boxes only look at the page in front of you

On the Lab queue and on Results, the free-text search box narrows the rows already on screen rather than searching the whole table — a patient on page 3 will not be found from page 1. Both screens say so above the table when it matters. The date range, the tab, and the Lab queue's Visit # box are real searches over everything, so use those to narrow the set first.

Two lists deliberately have no pager: Pay Doctors › Ready to pay and Waiting on insurance show every doctor at once, because a doctor who is owed money must never be hidden behind a next page on payout day. Critical Alerts that nobody has acknowledged are shown in full for the same reason.

2.4 Dashboard and phone

Your dashboard shows summary cards for your role: today's visits and queue for reception, your claimed and unclaimed tests for the lab, money and exceptions for admin. Each card is a link. Admin chooses which cards each role sees under Dashboard settings.

Every screen works on a phone. The sidebar folds into the ☰ menu top-left, tables scroll sideways, and buttons are thumb-sized. Session timeouts are normal; sign in again and continue.

Under Personal, My Profile holds your name and contact details, and the Two-step sign-in panel where you set up a one-time code (2.2). My Payslips shows your own payslips only, and only once Admin has finalised the payroll run for that period — a run still being prepared shows nothing rather than a row of zeros.

Chapter 3

Reception

ReceptionAdminWhere: the Messages & Bookings and Front Desk groups of the sidebar

Reception is the front door and the cashier. Almost every patient touches your screens twice: on arrival, to register and pay, and again when they come back for their result. The centre of your day is the Reception Queue; the screen you will use most carefully is New Visit. Results themselves are released by the lab team, not by reception (3.8).

Search doesn't reach everywhere

The search box only looks inside the same four sections this page always shows — pending callbacks, walk-ins waiting, today, and the next 30 days. It will not find an appointment that is older, cancelled, or already completed. Use Visit Records (3.7) for those.

Every booking made on the public /schedule page lands here, one row per service. Bookings for a doctor who has no published schedule, and all home-service requests, arrive as pending callback: the patient has been told you will call within one working day to agree a time. Reception and Admin are also emailed about each one (never for a booking reception makes) — see Email Alerts, 5.2.

Beside the search box, a Source drop-down (All sources by default) filters the list by how the patient reached the clinic: Online booking, Patient portal, Website message, Phone call, Text message, Facebook / Messenger, Walk-in, Referral, Other, or Not recorded for a booking made before this question existed. Picking one switches the page to the flat, sortable list the same way typing a search or clicking Sort this list → does, and that flat list carries its own Source column. In the grouped view a card instead shows a small via … line under its status chip whenever a source is on file.

When online booking is paused

An admin can pause online booking (Admin Tools › Online Booking). An amber banner then reads Online booking is paused. at the top of this page, and patients who try to book on the website or in the portal are asked to call, text, or message reception instead — so expect more calls and Messenger chats. Book them yourself with + New appointment, which keeps working as normal. The slide-over hides the self-book QR while booking is paused, because it would only lead to the same notice.

  1. Work the Pending callback section first thing.

    Call the patient, agree the time, and confirm the booking.

  2. When a booked patient walks in, click Mark arrived on their row.

    Only a confirmed booking can be marked arrived. Grouped multi-service bookings show one button for all of them.

  3. Click + Start visit.

    It opens New Visit with the patient already picked. The button appears only after arrived and only if the booking has a patient record. A walk-in booking taken by phone has no record yet, so register the patient first (3.2) and start the visit from their page.

    When you save the visit, the appointment moves to Completed on its own — and so do the others in the same multi-service booking. Nothing else marks an appointment done, so always start the visit from the appointment row rather than from the patient's page when there is a booking: that is what keeps the Walk-ins waiting count on your dashboard honest.

Taking a booking by phone

  1. Click + New appointment in the page header.

    A panel slides in from the right. Its note says it: for a walk-in who is ready now, create the visit instead.

  2. Choose the patient mode: Existing, New or Walk-in.

    • Existing: search by name, phone or DRM-ID. Their upcoming bookings show so you don't double-book.
    • New: a short form. Email is needed so the system can match duplicates and send the confirmation.
    • Walk-in: just a name and phone. No patient record is created; you register them when they arrive.

  3. Answer How did they reach us?

    Required on every staff-made booking: Website message (for someone you're booking by hand after they wrote in, without using the inbox's own Book appointment button), Phone call, Text message, Facebook / Messenger, Walk-in, Referral or Other. Booking straight from a Website Message (3.11) fills this in and stamps its source for you.

  4. Pick the booking type and services.

    Diagnostic package, Lab request, Doctor appointment or Home service. Doctor appointments need a physician and a slot; packages and most lab tests need no time.

  5. Add Notes (optional) if there's anything reception or the doctor should know.

    Up to 2,000 characters — the box's placeholder reads Anything reception or the doctor should know. Every booking has this field, not just ones opened from a website message; booking straight from a message (3.11) pre-fills it with From website message: <subject> — using General enquiry in place of the subject when the message had none. A note also shows, truncated, under the patient's name on the Appointments list — hover it to read the whole thing.

  6. If an amber ⚠ Scheduling conflict box appears, read it, then either change the slot or click Book anyway.

    You may override closures, doctor hours and taken slots. The override is recorded with your name.

  7. Tick Send confirmation (SMS + email) if the patient wants one, then Create.

In practice

Confirmations go out by email. The SMS channel exists in the software but has never been switched on, so tell patients to watch their inbox, not their texts.

Opened from a Website Message

Clicking Book appointment on a message in the Website Messages inbox (3.11) opens this same panel with the sender's details filled in and a banner at the top: Booking for a website message from <name>. If the message had no phone number on file, it adds This message had no phone number on file — add one below before saving. Finishing the booking marks that message Booked on its own — you don't set that status by hand. Opening the panel for a message that's already booked shows a notice instead — That website message was already booked. — with a link to the message and its appointment.

Let patients register themselves

The Registration Link button in the Appointments header shows a QR code that opens the public /register form. Copy link to send it by chat, or Open printable poster → for a counter poster. Patients who register or book online arrive in your Patients list flagged Pre-registered · verify. Check their ID at the counter (3.3).

Why consent matters

Admin can switch on a consent gate that blocks result release for any patient without consent on file. It is off today, and only a handful of existing patients have consent recorded, so capture it at every registration and every first visit of a returning patient. It takes one tick.

3.4 Create the visit

A visit is one trip to the clinic. It bundles every test, package, consultation or procedure the patient is having today. If you open the screen without a patient it first asks you to pick one: search, click Start visit →, or + New patient to register.

  1. Confirm the Patient card at the top is the right person.

  2. Under Select services, tick each test, package, consult or procedure.

    • Use the Lab & Services tab for tests, imaging and packages; Doctor for consultations and procedures. Each tab shows a count of what you have ticked.
    • A package row lists what it Includes. Tick the package, not its parts.
    • There is no quantity box. A service is on the visit or it isn't.

  3. Apply Line discounts where they apply.

    Each line has a dropdown starting at No discount. The choices are whatever Admin set up under Discounts, including the statutory Senior / PWD 20%. A senior discount on a service that is not eligible for it is simply ignored, so you cannot over-discount by mistake.

  4. For an HMO patient, fill in the HMO block.

    There are two, because lab work and doctor fees are often approved separately: Lab & Services HMO and Doctor / PF HMO. Pick the Provider (the default is — Cash / no HMO —), then enter the Approval date and Authorization no. from the LOA. An HMO visit reaches the lab without a cash payment.

  5. For a consultation or procedure, pick the Attending physician and check the fee.

    • Consultation fee (₱) pre-fills from the doctor's default. It must be above zero.
    • Under a consultation, Clinic fee and Doctor's fee (PF) are pre-split from the doctor's settings. When the clinic fee starts at ₱0, the note beside them says why in plain words — the doctor rents space at the clinic, is a clinic shareholder, or has the clinic's share set to ₱0 on their profile. Both boxes can still be changed.
    • A procedure pre-fills its catalog price; you may type a Procedure description and an HMO approved (₱) amount.
    • The physician is required whenever the doctor earns a professional fee (PF) from the line. If you skip it the release will be blocked later, so pick it now.

  6. Click Create visit & issue PIN.

    The system assigns a visit number, creates one line per service, and issues a 60-day Secure PIN. You are taken to the receipt.

Doctor + lab on the same day

If you tick lines on both tabs, the system creates two visits that share one receipt: one for the doctor, one for the lab. They are folded together in the archive and the queue, but each has its own visit number and payment status. This keeps the doctor's fee separate from lab revenue.

Consult-only visits

A visit with only a consultation prints no receipt. You land on the visit page with a note explaining why. If the patient will need portal access (for a later lab visit on the same account they get a new PIN anyway), use Issue portal PIN on the visit page, which prints a portal-access slip with no charges on it.

3.5 The receipt and the Secure PIN

/staff/visits/[visit]/receipt · also Receipt on any visit page and Print billing in the queue
Shows
Clinic header · patient and DRM-ID · consent on file or not · every line and discount · totals · the Patient Portal Access box
Portal box
DRM-ID · Secure PIN · Sign in at drmed.ph/portal to view results when ready. PIN is valid for 60 days. Any PIN from an earlier visit also keeps working until it expires, so please destroy old slips.

  1. Print it straight away and hand it to the patient.

    The PIN stays on screen only until you click Print, or for five minutes, whichever comes first. After that the box reads Already viewed — re-issue from admin if needed. Nobody, including Admin, can look it up later.

  2. Point to the portal box and say the two things that matter.

    Your results will be at drmed.ph/portal. Sign in with this DRM-ID and this PIN. The PIN works for 60 days.

  3. If they lose it, they come back with an ID and you re-issue.

    Open the patient's page, click Re-issue PIN. A new PIN for their latest visit replaces the old one and prints on a portal-access slip. See 3.9.

Never

Never write the PIN anywhere else, read it over the phone to a caller you cannot identify, or send it by chat. Anyone holding the DRM-ID and PIN can read that patient's results. A PIN from an earlier visit is not automatically cancelled by a new one, so tear up or shred any old receipt once a fresh one is printed.

  2. Pick the right visit.

    A deleted payment cannot be brought back.

Book at drmed.ph/schedule

6.3 Privacy consent (RA 10173)

The clinic asks for your consent once, under the Data Privacy Act of 2012, before processing your health information. You can give it four ways:

  • by signing the printed form at reception, or signing on screen at the counter;
  • by ticking the privacy box when you register at drmed.ph/register;
  • by ticking the required Service agreement when you book at drmed.ph/schedule — this counts as consent only if the booking is what creates your record;
  • by accepting the notice the first time you sign in to the portal.

Either online box records the same thing: consent given by you, on that date, against the current version of the notice. If the clinic already has a record for you, the public forms deliberately do not touch your consent — only you at the counter or in the portal can change it. You can withdraw it at any time by telling reception. Every view and download of your results is logged, and the clinic never sends results by text message.

Common questions

I can't find a patient.
Search by phone number or DRM-ID rather than name. Try one word at a time. Patients who booked online may be listed with their online spelling; check the Pre-registered · verify rows.
Someone messaged through the website. Where do I see it?
Messages & Bookings › Website Messages (3.11) — never by SMS, and never in your dashboard's Appointments card. The sidebar badge counts unanswered ones. Reply right there in the message's Reply panel (email or text) — sending marks it Replied for you — or call using the links on the message and click Mark replied yourself.
The test isn't on the lab queue.
The visit is Waiting for payment. Record the payment and it appears at once. If the visit is HMO-billed it should already be there — check the HMO was actually picked on the visit, not just written on the form.
I'm reception and a test line has no Print result button.
Reception sees every test line on the bill, but can print only what is released. The line is still with the lab, or it shares one report with a test that isn't released yet (Part of or Rest of this report isn't released yet), or the lab has no file on record (No PDF on file). Ask the lab; releasing stays their job.
The Release button is greyed out.
Hover it: it says either Visit must be paid, waived, or HMO-covered before release or Patient consent not on file — capture consent first. Reception fixes that, then you release. If the message mentions an attending physician, reception assigns one on the visit. An HMO visit is never blocked for money.
I claimed the wrong test.
Unclaim it yourself — the row's Unclaim in the queue, or on the test page: Your claim › Unclaim — put it back in the queue › Confirm unclaim. Only while nothing has been uploaded to it; after that, ask an Admin.
The patient wants a printed result at the counter.
Once the result is released, reception clicks Print result on the visit page or under Lab & Imaging › Queue (Released today). For an earlier day, change the dates on Released today. If it is not released yet, the lab has to release it first.
The patient lost their PIN.
Open the patient's page, click Re-issue PIN, print the slip. The old PIN stops working. Never read a PIN over the phone.
I recorded a payment on the wrong visit.
Click Move beside it and pick the right visit, even if it belongs to another patient. Nothing needs recording again.
I picked the wrong payment method (or typed the wrong amount).
Edit the payment on the visit page. The old version stays in the history with your reason.
I need to remove a test that was billed by mistake.
If unpaid: Delete the line with a reason. If paid: delete the payment first, delete the line, then record the payment again for the corrected total.
A result is wrong and it's already released.
The lab clicks Undo on the line, amends with a reason, and releases again. Reception phones the patient if they had already opened it.
The chemistry form is all greyed out.
Template configuration problem. Tell Admin; don't work around it.
End of Day won't close.
A non-zero Difference needs a Reason for the difference. Also check the ₱20 bill and ₱20 coin rows are in the right columns.
The doctor's fee isn't showing under Pay Doctors.
Admin or the pathologist must click Mark consultation done on the visit (reception can see the doctor line but cannot mark it done; medtechs do not see it at all), and the visit must be paid, waived or HMO-billed. Once marked done, an HMO visit's fee waits under Waiting on insurance until the claim settles.
My phone shows no sidebar.
Tap ☰ at the top left.
The system says my session expired.
Sign in again. Draft results you saved are still there.
Chapter 8

Known limitations (September 2026)

Things the screens hint at but don't do yet, so nobody wastes time looking for them.

Reception and results
Reception sees the test lines and their prices on a visit — it enters and collects that bill — and, once a line is released, its PDF, so it can print the patient's copy. It never sees a result before release, and it has no Release, Undo or Mark-done buttons. Releasing is the lab's job. (Reception saw no test lines at all between May and September 2026, and no released PDFs until 24 September 2026.)
Doctor lines
Consultation and procedure lines are visible only to Admin and pathologists, so only they can click Mark consultation done. Reception can still assign the physician and record the fee.
HMO package headers
On an HMO-billed package the component tests release normally, but the package's own summary line stays at ready for release: it is released automatically only when the visit's payment status changes, and there is no button anywhere to release it by hand. It clears when Admin records the settlement. Admin's Stuck Tests report lists these under Package header not auto-released.
HMO balances look unpaid
An HMO visit keeps its unpaid payment status until Admin records the settlement — that is correct, the insurer really does still owe it. It does not sit on the Reception Queue's Waiting for payment tab any more, and the queue shows it as HMO · provider rather than a red Unpaid badge. Do not waive the balance to tidy the list — that writes off a real receivable (3.6).
Pathologist sign-off
The Sign-off page is a placeholder and no service has Requires pathologist sign-off switched on. Results go from the bench straight to ready for release, and the pathologist dashboard's two sign-off counters therefore read zero. Pathologists review via Critical Alerts and amendments instead.
SMS
No text messages are sent to patients. Booking confirmations, reminders and result-ready notices are email only. Patients with no email get no automatic notice.
A lost authenticator cannot be cleared in the app
Nothing in the app can remove an enrolled authenticator, and Google sign-in does not bypass the code once you're enrolled — there is no self-service reset, no Admin reset, and no recovery codes. Clearing a lost factor needs direct access to the Supabase project. Treat enrolment as a decision, not an experiment.
Doctor procedures
The catalog has no Doctor procedure services yet, so that path is untested in daily use. Add them under Services before billing procedures.
Report exports are capped
Every report CSV stops at 20,000 rows and marks itself TRUNCATED at the end; most report pages show only the first 500 rows on screen. Narrow the date range rather than assuming that is everything (5.3).
Marketing trackers
Stored in the browser that entered them. They don't sync between computers.
Parked pages
Sell Gift Code, Refund Gift Code Sale, Registration Link, Sign-off and Patient Receivables (Aging) are under Hidden Tabs (Admin only in the sidebar). Reception can still open Sell Gift Code, Refund Gift Code Sale and Registration Link by typing the address.
Chapter 9

Glossary

Visit
One trip to the clinic. Bundles every test, consult or procedure done that day. Has a visit number and a payment status. A doctor-plus-lab day is two visits on one receipt.
Visit #
The running number per visit, printed on the receipt. Search it with or without leading zeros.
DRM-ID
The patient's permanent ID, e.g. DRM-0001.
Secure PIN
8 characters, issued per visit, valid 60 days, printed once. With the DRM-ID it opens the portal. Sign-in accepts any of a patient's unexpired PINs, not only the newest.
Pre-registered
A patient who registered or booked online and hasn't shown an ID at the counter yet.
Lab gate
The rule that hides unpaid, non-HMO visits from the lab bench.
Release
Making a finished result visible to the patient. Needs a visit that is paid, waived or billed to an HMO.
Waived
Admin decided not to collect the balance. Counts as paid.
Void
The accounting word for Delete on a payment: cancelled with a reason, the record stays, the balance re-opens. An Edit that changes the method or amount voids the old payment and records the corrected one in the same step.
Package
A bundle of tests sold at one price. On a visit it is a ₱0 header line with the tests underneath.
Report group
Several tests reported on one form and one PDF: the chemistry panel.
Send-out
A test run by a partner lab. The medtech uploads the partner's PDF.
HMO
The patient's health plan, chosen on the visit. An HMO visit reaches the lab and releases while still showing unpaid; the balance is a receivable the clinic collects from the plan later, never from the patient.
PF
Professional fee: the doctor's share of a consultation or procedure. Accrues when Admin or the pathologist marks the line done; Admin pays it out.
Clinic cut
The clinic's share of a consultation fee for PF-split doctors.
Attending physician
The doctor recorded on a visit. Required whenever a line earns PF.
Critical alert
An automatic flag when a finalised value crosses a critical threshold. Acknowledged by a pathologist or admin.
Amendment
A correction to a finalised result, with a reason. The original is kept.
EOD
End of Day: counting the drawer by denomination and closing the shift.
Audit Log
The tamper-evident record of every action, view, print and download. Required by RA 10173.
Online Booking pause
An admin switch that replaces the online booking form with a contact reception notice. Staff booking is unaffected.
Consent Gate
An admin switch that blocks release for patients without consent on file. Off today.
Email Alerts
The Admin Tools page (5.2) that controls who gets the new-website-message, new-online-booking, result-template-problem and possible-duplicate-patients alert emails — on/off per alert, staff switched in or out, and extra addresses.
Website message
A message sent through the site's Contact form, tracked in the Website Messages inbox (3.11) until it's replied to, booked or closed.
Reply (website message)
An email or text sent from inside a website message's Reply panel (3.11), to the sender's own address or number only. Kept in that message's Reply history; sending one moves a New message to Replied automatically.
Referral source (How did you hear about us?)
Where a patient first heard about the clinic — Facebook, Google, a doctor, a friend and so on. Stored once on the patient record: reception picks it on New Patient, and new patients answer it themselves on the website booking and registration forms. Counted in Booking Sources as New patients by how they heard about us. Not the same as Source below, which is how one appointment was made.
Source (How did they reach us?)
How a patient reached the clinic for an appointment: online booking, the patient portal, a website message, phone, text, Messenger, walk-in, referral or other. Shown as via … on an appointment card and as a Source column and filter on Appointments (3.1).
